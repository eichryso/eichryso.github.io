A comprehensive search for methanamine in space

  • Astrochemistry
  • Interstellar medium
  • ALMA archival data

Methanamine is a molecule of particular interest in the interstellar medium, because it may be an important intermediate species in the formation of amino acids, which are thought to play a major role in forming the building blocks of life. While methanamine has not been commonly looked for and only a handful of detections have been published, it is in fact very abundant in certain environments. This project aims to do a survey of sources where methanamine is detected, using the ALMA archival data. This way we can increase the number of known sources and improve our understanding about the total behavior of the molecule in various astronomical environments.

Unveiling planetary diversity: a statistical approach for classifying planetary systems

  • Exoplanets
  • Population synthesis
  • Machine learning

Planetary systems present remarkable diversity, yet the underlying processes that shape this diversity remain poorly understood. The prevailing theory suggests that planets form within circumstellar disks, where gas and dust coagulate into planetary bodies. Planetary population synthesis models attempt to replicate this process by simulating planet formation under varying physical conditions. Such models need to incorporate proper physical and initial conditions. Even with these assumptions, comparison with the observations is problematic. The observed exoplanetary population is affected by both the intrinsic trends and the biases from the selection effects of the different observing techniques. The aim of this project is to quantify how the selection effects influence the observed population and attempt to construct an intrinsic, unbiased, exoplanetary population. Based on our approach, we can test the existing theories for planet formation by directly comparing simulated planet distributions coming from planet population synthesis models with the intrinsic diversity of exoplanetary systems coming from the observations.

Exploring the Pistol star ejection cross section with Monte Carlo simulations through AMUSE

  • Stellar dynamics
  • N-body scattering
  • AMUSE
Paper in progressCode on GitHub

The Pistol star is one of the most massive stars in the Milky Way, with an estimated mass between 100 and 200 solar masses. Located close to the Galactic Center, adjacent to the Quintuplet cluster, high-resolution infrared observations of the Pistol star’s nebula have revealed evidence for its ejection from the cluster. We perform scattering simulations of three equal-mass binaries, with each star at 50 solar masses, to demonstrate the ejection scenario and constrain the formation history of the Pistol star. The results indicate that successive hierarchical collisions in the presence of already massive stars would efficiently assemble stars exceeding 90 solar masses, followed by their ejection with velocities easily reaching 70 km/s. The simulations yield results consistent with the Pistol star’s observed velocity of 60–95 km/s.

Stellar structure and evolution of a 2 solar mass main-sequence star

  • Stellar evolution
  • MESA

We aim to solve for the evolution of a 2 solar mass star using the MESA stellar evolution code. Our numerical simulations should confirm the theory, and our results should describe the general evolution of the star from the pre-main sequence (PMS) to white dwarf (WD), including its different evolutionary stages and the energy transport mechanism in the PMS and main-sequence (MS) phases.

Procedural content generation in Minecraft

  • Procedural generation
  • Game AI
Code on GitHub

For the course Modern Game AI Algorithms, we applied procedural content generation (PCG) to generate a house in Minecraft. While this is a relatively simple task, it entails some challenges, as randomness and believability are two important yet contradicting aspects of the assignment. Stochasticity offers great variation in the generated outcomes, which many PCG tasks need, but it comes at the cost of controllability. To simplify the problem, we focused on the core aspects of generating a house and explored various PCG methods to address them.
